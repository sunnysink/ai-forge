Fire Linktree This Weekend

Keep the QR code on your printed cards. Own the page it opens. Stop paying the monthly rent.

A weekend build · no reprinting · free forever once it’s done

Here is the trap a lot of practitioners are stuck in. You printed business cards with a QR code that opens your Linktree. Now you keep paying the monthly fee because you think cancelling turns every card you handed out into a dead link.

You can keep every card working and still cancel. The whole job is pointing the same web address at a page you own instead of a page you rent. Your own page can be free, prettier, and fully yours. This walks you through it start to finish.

Start here · two minutes

Scan your own card first

Everything depends on one thing: what your printed QR code actually opens. Pick up a card, open your phone camera, and scan it. Read the web address at the top of the page it loads. It will be one of two kinds, and they take different routes.

Green light

It opens a domain you own

Something like yourname.com or links.yourname.com. You bought this domain, so you control where it points.

You can do the whole thing. Repoint the domain to your own page and cancel Linktree completely. This is the paid Linktree feature you’ve been renting, and it’s the exact case this guide is built for.

Read this one

It opens linktr.ee/yourname

The address starts with linktr.ee/. That address lives on Linktree’s servers, not yours, so you can’t move it off them.

The printed cards stay on Linktree’s free plan. Downgrade to free (no charge, that link keeps working), build your own page anyway, and point every new card, bio, and post at your page going forward. See the note at the bottom of the build.

The quiet win most people miss If your card opens a domain you own, the monthly fee you’re paying is the custom-domain feature. That is the only thing Linktree is doing for you that free can’t. Move the domain to your own page and there is nothing left to pay for.

One thing before you start

You’re only replacing one page

The thing you’re building is your link-in-bio page: the page your QR code opens, the same one your Instagram bio and your emails point to. That’s the page that’s living on Linktree right now, and it’s the only page you’re touching today.

If you also have a regular website A separate site that shows up when someone Googles “tarot reading near me” is a different thing, and you leave it completely alone. Rewriting a page that already ranks in Google is how you lose the ranking. Your link-in-bio page and your website do two different jobs, so keep both. This guide only moves the link-in-bio page off Linktree.

The build · a weekend

Build the page you own

This is the green-light path: your card opens a domain you own. Free hosting, your own design, and the same web address your cards already carry.

  1. Build your link-in-bio page with Claude

    Ask Claude to make you a one-page link-in-bio page in your brand: your name, a short line about what you do, your photo, and a button for each place you send people (booking, your search site, email list, socials, any offer). Ask for it as a single file called index.html.

    Non-negotiable The file must be named index.html, all lowercase. Index.html or INDEX.HTML breaks the hosting and returns a 404.

  2. Host it free on GitHub Pages

    Create a free GitHub account. Make a new repository, add your index.html, and in the repo go to Settings → Pages and turn Pages on for the main branch. In about a minute you get a live web address like yourname.github.io. Open it. That’s your page, live on the internet, at no cost.

  3. Point your domain at it

    Now the piece that keeps your cards alive. You’re telling your domain to open your new page instead of Linktree. Two routes, pick one.

    Route A: connect the domain to GitHub (the clean one). In your repo’s Settings → Pages → Custom domain, type your domain and save. GitHub shows you the exact records to add. Then log in where your domain lives (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare, wherever you bought it) and add them.

    For a subdomain like links.yourname.com, one CNAME record:

    Type CNAME Name links Value yourname.github.io

    For the bare domain yourname.com, four A records pointing at GitHub:

    Type A Value 185.199.108.153 Type A Value 185.199.109.153 Type A Value 185.199.110.153 Type A Value 185.199.111.153
    Always trust the screen over this page GitHub prints the exact records for your domain right there in the Pages settings. Those published values are the source of truth. Copy them from that screen, not from memory.

    Route B: a plain forward (the no-fuss one). Not ready to touch DNS records? Every domain seller has a “Forwarding” or “Redirect” setting. Point your domain to forward to your yourname.github.io address. It’s a few clicks and no records. The one tradeoff: the visitor may briefly see the github.io address in their bar. For a QR code nobody reads, that’s fine.

  4. Wait a minute, then scan your card

    DNS changes and GitHub Pages both take a short while to go live. A fresh custom domain can take up to a few minutes, sometimes longer the very first time. If your page 404s right after you save, it isn’t broken. It’s still building. Wait a minute, hard-refresh, and give a brand-new custom domain up to an hour before you worry.

    Then pick up a printed card and scan it with your phone, the same way a client would. When it opens your new page, the cards are safe.

  5. Cancel Linktree

    Your cards point at a page you own. Go into Linktree billing and cancel. Nothing you printed breaks, and the monthly charge stops. Done.

If your card opens linktr.ee/yourname You can’t move that address, so don’t force it. Downgrade Linktree to the free plan, which keeps that printed link working at no charge, and cancel only the paid part. Build your own page anyway and point every new card, every bio, and every post at it. Over time the printed cards age out and your page quietly takes over. You still stop paying today.

The five-second version

What you’re actually doing

  • The card points at a web address. That never changes.
  • You change where that address goes: a page you own, not one you rent.
  • Your own page is free, prettier, and yours.
  • The rent stops. Linktree Pro is usually around fifteen dollars a month. That’s a hundred and eighty a year you keep.

Make it yours
