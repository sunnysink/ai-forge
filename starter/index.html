Claude Code Starter

Give Claude a permanent memory of your business, so you stop re-explaining yourself every single time you open a new chat.

Runs in your browser. Nothing to install on your computer. You will not write code.

What you'll have at the end

A folder your assistant actually reads

Right now, everything Claude knows about your business lives inside one conversation. Close the tab and it's gone. You paste your voice, your offers, and your ideal client back in every time, and you have gotten very good at it, which is the sad part.

By the end of this guide you'll have a private folder holding your brand voice, your offers, and your client notes, and an assistant that opens it before answering you. A filing cabinet instead of a sticky note.

Read this before you start, so nothing surprises you four steps in.

You'll need a Claude plan at Pro or above. Claude Code in the browser isn't available on the free plan. If you're on free right now, everything below still makes sense to read, and you'll need to upgrade before Step 3 will work.

You'll need a GitHub account, which is free to make.

Time: about 20 minutes, and most of that is waiting on signups.
Step One

Get a GitHub account

GitHub is where your folder is going to live. Think of it as a filing cabinet that sits online instead of on your desk, so it's there whether you're on your laptop, your phone, or someone else's computer.

  • Go to github.com/signup
  • Use whatever email you actually check
  • Slow down on the username. Read the box below before you pick it.
Your username becomes part of every public link you ever make here.
Anything you publish from GitHub gets an address built out of it: yourname.github.io/the-thing/. Every artifact, tracker, quiz, and client tool you build carries that name in the link, and the only way around it is buying a domain and pointing it there each separate time.

So pick it the way you'd pick a business email. Your business name or your own name both work beautifully. The handle you made in 2009 is going to look strange in a link you send a paying client.
Already have an account? You're set, carry on to the next step.
Step Two

Copy your filing cabinet

In GitHub, a filing cabinet is called a repository. Ugly word, simple thing. It's a folder that can hold other folders.

You don't have to build yours. It already exists as a template, with every folder and file already named and waiting. You're making your own copy of it.

  • Open the Claude Code Starter Kit
  • Click the green Use this template button, then Create a new repository
  • Name it biz-brain, lowercase, with the hyphen
  • Leave Include all branches unchecked
  • Click Private. This matters. Your client notes and your offers are in here.
  • Click Create repository
Do not skip Private. Public means anyone on the internet can read it, including people who found it by accident. Private means you and whoever you invite. If you clicked past it, open Settings on the repo and change it now.
What you just got. Empty labeled files, nothing filled in: your voice, your offers, who you are, who you serve, plus a CLAUDE.md holding the standing instructions Claude reads at the start of every session. The names matter more than they look like they do. Every lesson after this one says things like "put that in your brand folder," and that has to mean one thing.
Step Three

Install the Claude app on your GitHub

This is the step where Claude gets permission to open your files. Takes about a minute.

"Install" here does not mean install anything on your computer. You're installing the Claude app onto your GitHub account, and it all happens in your browser. Nothing downloads, nothing lands on your machine. Follow the exact words below and you'll land in the right place.
  • Go to claude.ai/code and sign in with your Claude account
  • On "Setup and start coding," click "want to set up apps later, continue on the web." It sits below the bigger buttons, so look down the page for it.
  • Click Get Started
  • Click "connect a different way," then Sign in with GitHub
  • GitHub asks which account to install on. Pick the one that owns biz-brain.
  • GitHub asks which repositories. Choose All repositories, or Only select repositories and pick biz-brain.
  • Click Install

You may be asked to create a cloud environment. If you are, the defaults are right. Give it any name and click Create environment. If you're never asked, nothing is wrong, that screen doesn't always appear.

Back at claude.ai/code, click the repository selector under the message box and choose biz-brain.

This is the step that needs Pro. If you're on the free plan you'll get stopped here, and it's the plan, not anything you did wrong.

No repositories in the list?

This is the most common wall, and it's almost always the same cause: Claude knows who you are, but it was never given the key to your files. Logging in with your GitHub account and installing the Claude app on that account are two separate things, and it's easy to do the first and stop. Nothing warns you. The list just comes back empty.

Install it directly:

  • github.com/apps/claude/installations/new

Choose the account that owns your repo, choose All repositories or select biz-brain, click Install, then reload claude.ai/code. Your repo will be there.

A private repo is not the problem. Private works exactly like public here, as long as the app is installed on the account that owns it.
Don't ask Claude to fix a connection problem. Claude can't install a GitHub app or change your plan from inside a conversation. It will sound like it's helping, and nothing will move. This one is yours to click.
Buttons move around as things get updated. If a label reads a little differently than what's written here, you're still in the right place. Look for the word connect or repository and follow it.
Step Four

Fill the drawers

Empty folders do nothing. This is the step that turns a tidy structure into an assistant that knows you.

Open a normal Claude conversation, the regular chat and not Claude Code, and paste this:

You've been helping me with my business for a while. I'm building a permanent folder of files so you don't start from scratch every conversation. Using only what you actually know about me, write me ONE file called business-brain-dump.md, with these four sections inside it: who-i-am My story. What I do, how I got here, what I did before this. who-i-serve My ideal client. Who they are, and what's going on for them when they find me. voice How I sound. Phrases that are mine. Words and tones I'd never use. offers What I sell. Name, price, and what's different for the person afterward. Important: - Use only what you genuinely know about me. Do not fill gaps with reasonable guesses. - Anywhere you don't actually know something, write [FILL IN] and move on. I would much rather have a visible gap than a sentence that sounds right and isn't true. - Write it in my voice where you can tell what my voice is. Give me that one file in ONE markdown block. One block, one copy. Do not split it into separate blocks, separate documents, downloads, or attachments. After the block, in a few lines outside it, tell me which of the four sections came out thin and what's actually missing from each. I want to know where to spend my time.
Why one file and not four. Ask for four files and Claude tends to hand you four separate boxes, which is four copies and four chances to lose one. One file with four sections inside it comes back as a single block. Claude Code does the splitting in the next step, which is the part it's good at.

Copy the whole block. Go back to Claude Code and paste it in with this underneath:

If it still splits the answer into separate boxes, say "put the whole thing in one markdown block so I can copy it in one go" and it'll redo it.
Above is a brain dump with four sections in it. Split it into the matching files in this repo, replacing the placeholders: who-i-am goes into context/who-i-am.md who-i-serve goes into context/who-i-serve.md voice goes into brand/voice.md offers goes into offers/offers.md Keep the text exactly as written, including every [FILL IN] marker. Don't tidy them up or fill them in. Then push to main.
Claude may mention it can't find business-brain-dump.md in the repo. That's expected. You pasted the text rather than committing a file, so there's nothing to look for and nothing to delete. It splits from what you pasted.
Then read what it wrote. Every [FILL IN] is a real gap, and anything that isn't marked is something Claude believes about your business. Check the prices. Check the offer names. A wrong fact in here quietly becomes a wrong fact in everything it writes for you afterward.
Step Five

Make the holes chase you

You will not have filled everything in. Nobody does. There are [FILL IN] markers scattered through those files right now, and left alone they'll still be there in March.

So don't rely on remembering. Build the reminder into the folder itself.

Go through every file in this repo and find every gap left for me: [FILL IN] markers, any text still sitting inside square brackets, and any section that's still a placeholder rather than real information about my business. Create a file at the root called open-questions.md: - Turn each gap into a plain question I can actually answer, not a label. "What does the Premium tier cost?" rather than "[FILL IN: price]". - Put the file it belongs in next to each question. - Use checkboxes so I can watch the list shrink. - Order them by how much they're holding back. The gaps that block the most of your future work go at the top. Add one line at the top explaining that order. Then add this standing rule to CLAUDE.md: At the start of every session, check open-questions.md. If anything is still unanswered, tell me how many are left and ask me exactly ONE of them, starting from the top. When I answer, write it into the right file, tick it off in open-questions.md, and then carry on with whatever I actually came in to do. Then push to main.
Why one question and not the list. A list of fourteen questions gets closed and ignored. One question gets answered in thirty seconds while you're already here for something else. Answer one a day and the whole thing is done in two weeks without ever sitting down to do it.
Why the order matters. A missing price breaks every sales email you'll ever ask for. A missing origin story breaks one about page. Same effort to answer, very different payoff, so the list puts the expensive gaps first.
One trick for when it asks about your voice. Don't describe how you sound. Paste in three things you've actually written that felt like you, and let it work out the pattern. Describing your own voice is genuinely hard. Showing it is easy.
This is the part that keeps working after you close the tab. Everything up to here was setup you did once. This is the folder getting better on its own, every time you come back, without you managing it.
