Why we're here

You built the brain. This is the morning it was for.

Last time, you gave Claude a permanent memory of your business. The files are there, and it reads them before it answers. Today we turn that into a morning brief: you sit down, say good morning, and the whole day is already in front of you.

No more opening a blank tab and re-explaining yourself before you can even start.

A morning brief is your Forge Director in action. One short conversation at the start of the day. It reads everything it can reach and hands you the picture: what is on your calendar, what is due, which client is owed something, what you said last week you wanted to get to, and the ideas you did not want to lose.

You already have the hard part, the business brain. Today we add two things to it: the tools it reads from, and your instructions for what a good morning looks like.

What it reads each morning

Four sources, one picture

Your brief is only as good as what it can see. Here is everything it pulls from, and you decide which of these you turn on.

Your files The business brain you already built. Clients, projects, offers, your voice. It opens them first, every time.
Your calendar Connect it once and your real day shows up: what is booked, what is due, what is about to sneak up on you.
Your inbox and drive The email that came in overnight, the docs you keep your work in. It can reach the tools you already live in.
Your instructions You tell it what a good morning looks like, and it follows that every day without being re-asked.

Follow along

Five steps, and the hard part is already done

This one assumes you finished the Claude Code Setup and your biz-brain repo is filled in. If that is you, everything here is a short conversation or a couple of clicks.

No business brain yet? You can still build a morning brief in Claude Cowork. It is the easiest place to start if code is not your thing. It works from Google Drive instead of a repo: it reads your calendar and a Morning Brief folder, and writes each day's brief as a new dated Google Doc you can open from your phone. In Step Three, tap the Cowork option and the prompt rewrites itself for you.
Tick each step off as you finish it. Your progress shows in the sidebar and saves on this device, so if you get pulled away you will know exactly where you stopped.
Step One

Connect your calendar

This is the one that turns a nice summary into a real day. Connect your calendar once and Claude can read what is actually booked, instead of guessing.

  • In Claude, open Settings, then Connectors
  • Turn on Google Calendar, or whichever calendar you live in
  • Sign in when it asks, and allow it to read your calendar
Read access is enough. It is reading your day back to you, not moving anything around. Nothing on your calendar changes.
Step Two

Connect the rest of your world

Your calendar is the anchor. Then add the tools you already open every day, so the brief reaches your real work and not only your files.

In the same Connectors screen, turn on the ones you use:

  • Your email, so it can tell you what came in overnight
  • Your drive or docs, so it can reach the files you keep your work in
  • Whatever else you use daily that Claude offers a connector for

Then, and this is the part nearly everyone skips, open each connector's settings and check them. A connector can read as "connected" and still not have access to the calendar or the folder you actually need.

Do not breeze past the settings. This is the one that comes back to bite you: switched on, but never actually granted access to the right calendar or the right folder. Open each connector, confirm it can reach what you want, before you move on. Two minutes now saves you finding out at 6am tomorrow that the brief came back empty.
You do not need all of them. Start with your calendar and one more, and pick the tools you open every single day. If you rarely check it, it does not belong in your morning.
Step Three

Build your morning brief

This is the heart of it. Pick where you are building, tick what you want your morning to hold, and the prompt below writes itself. Copy it into your tool and it sets up whatever is needed, writes your brief, and builds you a one-page artifact to read it on.

Where are you building?

What do you want in your morning?
About your projects. You do not need a perfect list to start. Brain-dump what you are working on now, a few ideas, a few things you want to get to. Then as new things come up you tell Claude, and they get added. The list fills itself in over the first week.
Step Four

Run your first brief and check it over

Before the first run, a minute on the settings that decide how hands-off this is:

  • The model. Check you are routed to the model you want it running on.
  • Automatic approvals. Your call. You can approve each action yourself, or let it approve on its own. If you want the brief written and waiting when you open your computer in the morning, the more automatic path is what gets you there. If you would rather see each step first, keep it manual.
  • The time (for the Cowork task). Confirm it is set to run at the hour you actually want each morning.

Now run it. In Claude Code, say good morning. In Cowork, run the task or ask it to make today's brief. Then actually read what it hands you, because this first one is a draft of your morning, not the final version. In Cowork, open the scheduled task too and confirm it looks the way you want.

Then shape it. React out loud:

  • Too long? Tell it what to cut.
  • Missing something? Tell it what you always want to see.
  • Wrong tone? Tell it how you want to be met in the morning.
Every correction, ask it to save for next time. In Claude Code it writes the change into your brief file and pushes it. In Cowork, ask it to remember the change going forward. Either way, tomorrow's brief already has it, and it gets sharper while you do nothing but read and react.
Step Five

Make it your morning

That is the whole build. From here it is a habit. In Claude Code you say good morning and it reads your live calendar and hands you the day. In Cowork the scheduled task writes it for you each morning. You read the day and make your decisions, ten minutes, and your business is out of your head and in front of you.

One conversation a day. Your business stops living in your head and starts living somewhere you actually trust.
After today

The brief grows with you

You built the core in one sitting. From here it gets richer one small thing at a time, whenever you notice a gap. None of this is homework. It is the list to reach for when you want more.

What people add over the following weeks

One small thing at a time

  1. Add another connector when you want the brief to see more. Your email, your payments, whatever tool you start wishing it knew about. Turn it on, check its settings, and it is in tomorrow's brief.
  2. Point it at more of your files. A new client, a new project, a new ideas file. The brief reads whatever you give it, so growing the brain grows the brief.
  3. Feed it as things come up. Pop in any time and say "add this to my projects" or "put this in my ideas." A new client, an idea you do not want to lose, a decision you just made. In the morning brief or outside it, whenever it happens. Keeping the brain current is what keeps the brief worth reading.
